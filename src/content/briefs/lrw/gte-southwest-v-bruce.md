## Facts
A GTE supervisor regularly terrorized the employees he managed over several years: screaming and cursing at them, charging at them with fists clenched, forcing them to stand at attention and perform humiliating tasks, and threatening their jobs. Three employees developed stress-related conditions and sought treatment.

## Procedural posture
The employees sued GTE for intentional infliction of emotional distress and won a jury verdict. The court of appeals affirmed; the Supreme Court of Texas affirmed.

## Issue
Whether the supervisor's course of conduct was extreme and outrageous, and whether the employees' distress was severe.

## Holding
Yes to both. The evidence was legally sufficient on each element.

## Reasoning
The court restated the high bar for element two and warned that ordinary employment disputes never meet it. But the supervisor's behavior was not criticism or discipline; it was a regular pattern of threats, physical intimidation and humiliation that, taken as a whole, exceeded all bounds of decency. On severity, the symptoms, treatment and expert testimony sufficed. The losing argument, that each incident alone was minor, failed because the conduct is evaluated cumulatively.

## Rule (own words)
Look at the whole course of conduct and its context; outrageousness is about the character of the acts, not the defendant's motive; severity is proved with concrete evidence of symptoms and treatment.

## What-ifs
- If the supervisor had only criticized performance harshly, element two would fail.
- If the employees had shown only embarrassment, severity would fail.
