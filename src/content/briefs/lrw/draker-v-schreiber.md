## Facts
Two high school students created a fake MySpace profile in the name of their vice principal, using her name and photograph and adding false statements and sexual content. She sued the students and their parents for defamation, intentional infliction of emotional distress and negligence.

## Procedural posture
The trial court granted summary judgment for the defendants on all claims. The San Antonio Court of Appeals affirmed; no petition for review was filed.

## Issue
Whether an IIED claim can proceed when the conduct complained of is the publication of statements that injure the plaintiff's reputation.

## Holding
No. The gravamen of the complaint was defamation, so IIED was unavailable under Hoffmann-La Roche v. Zeltwanger, even though the defamation claim also failed.

## Reasoning
The court identified what the plaintiff was really complaining about: false and offensive statements about her, which is the domain of defamation. Under the gap-filler rule, IIED cannot substitute for that claim, and the plaintiff identified no conduct independent of the statements. That the defamation claim failed did not revive IIED.

## Rule (own words)
Content that hurts reputation is a defamation problem. IIED needs something else: independent conduct that is outrageous in itself.

## What-ifs
- If the students had also stalked or physically threatened her, that independent conduct could support IIED.
- The Marcus Carter facts add the group chat and the tournament comment; the question is whether those are more than reputational attacks.
