## Facts
An employee alleged that her supervisor subjected her to repeated sexual comments and conduct. She sued the employer for sexual harassment under the Texas Commission on Human Rights Act and for intentional infliction of emotional distress based on the same conduct.

## Procedural posture
The jury found for her on both theories. The court of appeals affirmed the IIED award. The Supreme Court of Texas reversed the IIED judgment.

## Issue
Whether an IIED claim may rest on the same conduct that forms the basis of a statutory sexual harassment claim.

## Holding
No. IIED is a gap-filler tort; where the gravamen of the complaint is sexual harassment, the statutory remedy governs and IIED is unavailable, whether or not the statutory claim succeeds.

## Reasoning
The tort was created to reach egregious conduct that no other remedy addressed. Allowing it to duplicate claims with their own limits (caps, procedures, defenses) would let plaintiffs evade those limits. The court looked at the gravamen of the complaint, found it was harassment, and held that only conduct independent of that claim could support IIED. None was shown.

## Rule (own words)
Ask first what the plaintiff is really complaining about. If another claim fits that core, IIED is off the table.

## What-ifs
- If the supervisor had also made death threats unrelated to the harassment, that independent conduct could support IIED.
- Draker v. Schreiber applies the same rule where the gravamen is defamation.
