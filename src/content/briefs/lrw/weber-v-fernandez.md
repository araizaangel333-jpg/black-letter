## Facts
The plaintiff, treated by the court as a public figure, sued several individuals over communications that, among other things, called him a robber. The defendants invoked the Texas Citizens Participation Act, which protects speech on matters of public concern by requiring early dismissal unless the plaintiff produces clear and specific evidence of each element of his claims.

## Procedural posture
The trial court denied the motion to dismiss; the defendants appealed. The court of appeals issued a memorandum opinion, with one justice concurring in part and dissenting in part on which communications were supported by sufficient evidence of falsity and actual malice.

## Issue
Whether the plaintiff presented clear and specific evidence of a prima facie case on each challenged claim.

## Holding
In part. The concurrence agreed that the plaintiff was a public figure, that the TCPA applied, and that there was clear and specific evidence that the statements calling him a robber were false, while disagreeing with the majority about other communications. (Read the majority opinion for the disposition of each claim, including any IIED claim.)

## Reasoning
The TCPA framework: step one, the movant shows the statute applies; step two, the nonmovant must show a prima facie case by clear and specific evidence; a public figure must also show actual malice. Verify how the court treated any claim pleaded alongside defamation.

## Rule (own words)
In Texas, a reputational claim against speech on a public matter has to show its evidence at the door, element by element, and a second tort pleaded for the same injury will be measured the same way.
