## Facts
De la Tour hired Hochster in April to accompany him as a courier on a European tour starting June 1. On May 11 he wrote that he had changed his mind and would not need Hochster. Hochster sued on May 22 and, before June 1, obtained another engagement starting July 4.

## Procedural posture
Verdict for Hochster; the defendant's objection that no action lay before June 1 was overruled.

## Issue
Whether a renunciation before the time for performance is a breach on which the promisee may sue immediately.

## Holding
Yes. The renunciation was a breach of the implied promise not to disable oneself from performing, and the promisee could treat the contract as ended and sue at once.

## Reasoning
Lord Campbell reasoned that it is more rational and in the interest of both parties that the injured party be free to find other employment and sue immediately, rather than remain idle and ready until the performance date; the defendant cannot complain of being sued sooner when he himself declared the contract over.

## Rule (own words)
When the other side says plainly that it will not perform, you do not have to wait: treat it as broken, cover your losses and sue.

## What-ifs
- De la Tour wrote that he was having second thoughts but would decide by June: not an unequivocal repudiation; Hochster could demand assurance.
- De la Tour retracted on May 20, before Hochster had sued or taken another job: retraction is effective if the other party has not materially changed position or indicated that he considers the repudiation final (R.2d § 256).
- Hochster waited until June 1 and then sued: also permitted; the question becomes mitigation from the date of repudiation.
