## Facts
Lindsell wrote to Adams offering wool and asking for an answer in course of post. The letter was misdirected and arrived late; Adams mailed an acceptance the day it arrived. Expecting an answer earlier, Lindsell sold the wool to someone else before the acceptance arrived.

## Procedural posture
Verdict for Adams; the court refused a new trial.

## Issue
When was the acceptance effective: on mailing or on receipt?

## Holding
On mailing. The sellers' own misdirection caused the delay, and a rule requiring receipt would lead to an endless exchange of confirmations.

## Reasoning
If acceptance required receipt, the offeror would need to confirm receipt, and the offeree that confirmation, and so on; the practical rule is that the contract is formed when the acceptance is put in the post.

## Rule (own words)
Once the yes is in the mail, the offeror is bound, even if he does not know it yet; revocation must arrive before that.
