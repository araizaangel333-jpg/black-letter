## Facts
John Ricketts handed his granddaughter Katie Scothorn a note for $2,000 payable on demand with interest, saying he had fixed things so she would not have to work any more. She quit her bookkeeping job. He paid some interest and expressed regret that he could not pay the principal before his death. The executor refused to pay.

## Procedural posture
Judgment for Katie; affirmed.

## Issue
Whether the note was enforceable despite lacking consideration.

## Holding
Yes, by estoppel. The grandfather intended her to rely on the note by giving up her employment, she did so, and it would be inequitable for the estate to deny the consideration.

## Reasoning
The court found no consideration: her quitting was not requested as an exchange. But equitable estoppel prevents a party who induced reliance from asserting a position inconsistent with the promise; having caused her to give up her job, the grandfather could not have pleaded want of consideration, and neither could his estate.

## Rule (own words)
A gift promise that was meant to change someone's life, and did, will be enforced against the promisor who caused the change.

## What-ifs
- Katie kept working and merely spent more: reliance not substantial or not intended; the note is a gift.
- The grandfather said: quit your job and I will give you $2,000: bargain; ordinary consideration.
- Today: R.2d § 90 enforces the promise or limits the remedy as justice requires; a court might award only reliance damages.
