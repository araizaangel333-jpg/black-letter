## Facts
Sailors and fishermen signed in San Francisco to work the salmon season in Alaska for a fixed wage. Once at the remote cannery, they refused to work unless paid about double; the company's superintendent, unable to replace them, signed new papers. At season's end the company paid only the original wage.

## Procedural posture
The men sued on the new agreement; they won below; the Ninth Circuit reversed.

## Issue
Whether the promise of higher wages was supported by consideration.

## Holding
No. The men promised nothing they were not already bound to do, so the new promise was gratuitous and the company's refusal to pay it was not a breach.

## Reasoning
The court treated the demand as taking advantage of the company's necessity, and refused to let a party profit from threatening to break his own contract.

## Rule (own words)
Doing what you already promised is not a price for a new promise. If the facts changed and the modification is fair, the modern rules may enforce it anyway.
