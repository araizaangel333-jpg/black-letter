## Facts
International Filter's salesman submitted a written proposal to Conroe Gin for a water-treatment filter. The document stated that it would become a contract when accepted by the purchaser and approved by an executive officer of the seller at its Chicago office. Conroe's manager signed the acceptance; the seller's president wrote O.K. on it with the date; the seller then sent a letter acknowledging the order and referring to shipment. Conroe later wrote to cancel; the seller refused the cancellation and sued.

## Procedural posture
Judgment for Conroe below on the theory that the seller's acceptance was never communicated; the Commission of Appeals (opinion adopted by the Supreme Court of Texas) reversed.

## Issue
Whether a contract was formed when the seller's executive officer approved the order, and whether notice of that approval to the buyer was required.

## Holding
A contract was formed on approval. The offer, as the parties framed it, made executive approval the act of acceptance and did not make communication of the approval a condition; even if it had, the acknowledgment letter supplied it.

## Reasoning
The seller's proposal, once signed by the buyer, was the buyer's offer on the seller's form. An offeror may dispense with notice of acceptance or specify its form. Here the document said approval by the seller's executive would make it a contract, and the president's O.K. was that approval. The subsequent letter, sent in the ordinary course, told the buyer the order was accepted; nothing more formal was required.

## Rule (own words)
Read the document to find who is offering and what counts as acceptance. If the offer makes the offeree's internal approval the acceptance, the deal closes when the approval happens, and the usual acknowledgment is all the notice anyone gets.

## What-ifs
- The president never wrote O.K. and the seller simply shipped: acceptance by performance, and shipment is notice.
- Conroe's cancellation reached the seller before the O.K.: revocation of the offer before acceptance; no contract.
- The proposal said acceptance must be confirmed by telegram: then the telegram is the required manner, and a letter might not do.
