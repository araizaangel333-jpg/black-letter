## Facts
Harvey telegraphed Facey, the owner of a property called Bumper Hall Pen: will you sell us Bumper Hall Pen? Telegraph lowest cash price. Facey replied: lowest price for Bumper Hall Pen £900. Harvey telegraphed back that they agreed to buy for £900 and asked for the title deed. Facey did not answer.

## Procedural posture
Harvey sued for specific performance. The trial court dismissed; the Court of Appeal of Jamaica found a contract; the Privy Council reversed and dismissed.

## Issue
Whether Facey's telegram stating the lowest price was an offer that Harvey's reply accepted.

## Holding
No. Facey answered only the question about price, not the question whether he would sell; the final telegram was an offer by Harvey that Facey never accepted.

## Reasoning
The Privy Council read the exchange question by question. The first telegram asked two things: willingness to sell and lowest price. The reply addressed price alone, and a statement of price is not a promise to sell at that price. Harvey's third telegram could therefore only be an offer, and silence did not accept it.

## Rule (own words)
Answering how much is not the same as saying yes. Match each reply to the question it answers before calling anything an offer.

## What-ifs
- Facey's reply: I will sell Bumper Hall Pen for £900 cash. Offer; Harvey's telegram accepts.
- Facey's reply quoted the price and added: reply by Friday. Closer to an offer, because a deadline suggests a commitment awaiting acceptance.
- Facey answered Harvey's third telegram with yes. Contract formed at that point.
