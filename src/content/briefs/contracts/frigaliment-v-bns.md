## Facts
A Swiss buyer contracted with a New York seller for "US Fresh Frozen Chicken, Grade A, Government Inspected, Eviscerated" in two weights. For the heavier birds the seller shipped stewing chickens (older fowl); the buyer insisted "chicken" meant young birds suitable for broiling and frying, protested, but accepted a second shipment and then sued.

## Procedural posture
Breach of warranty action tried to the court. Judge Friendly dismissed the complaint: the buyer had not carried its burden of proving that "chicken" was used in the narrower sense.

## Issue
Whether "chicken" in the contract meant only young broilers (the buyer's reading) or any bird of that species meeting the grade (the seller's reading), and who must prove which.

## Holding
Judgment for the seller. The buyer failed to prove the narrower meaning; the seller's reading was consistent with the regulatory definition the contract referenced and with the price.

## Reasoning
The word alone was ambiguous, so the court looked outward: (1) the contract invoked Department of Agriculture grading regulations whose definition of "chicken" includes stewing fowl; (2) the negotiations were in German, where "Huhn" covers both; (3) the buyer's witnesses on trade usage were unconvincing, and a usage binds a newcomer only if it is so general that knowledge can be presumed; (4) the contract price was below the market for broilers, so the seller could not reasonably have thought it was selling broilers; (5) the buyer accepted the second shipment. Since the seller's subjective meaning matched at least one objective meaning, and the buyer could not show the seller knew of the buyer's meaning, the buyer lost.

## Rule (own words)
Interpretation is a search through every available clue about what the parties meant, and the party pushing the unusual meaning must prove it. A newcomer is not bound by a trade usage it did not know unless the usage is nearly universal.

## What-ifs
- If the seller had known the buyer meant broilers, R.2d § 201(2) would bind the seller to the buyer's meaning.
- If the price had matched broiler prices, the court's reading of the seller's state of mind would flip.
