## Facts
Westside granted Humble an option, for a stated consideration, to buy a tract of land within a fixed period. Before the period ended, Humble wrote proposing that the contract be amended to require the seller to extend utility lines, stating the offer was otherwise unchanged; later, still within the period, Humble notified Westside that it exercised the option. Westside refused to convey, arguing that the request for utilities was a counteroffer that rejected and terminated the option.

## Procedural posture
Humble sued for specific performance. The trial court granted summary judgment for Westside and the court of civil appeals affirmed; the Supreme Court of Texas reversed and rendered judgment for Humble.

## Issue
Whether an optionee's proposal of additional terms terminates an option supported by consideration.

## Holding
No. The option was a binding contract to keep the offer open; Humble's proposal did not reject the option, and its later unconditional exercise within the period formed the contract of sale.

## Reasoning
The counteroffer rule exists because an ordinary offeror is free to revoke and may treat a counteroffer as a rejection; an option holder, by contrast, has bought the right to accept during the period, and a proposal to modify does not surrender that right unless the holder clearly says so. The exercise notice was unconditional and timely.

## Rule (own words)
If you paid for the time, you can shop for better terms and still take the deal you paid to hold.

## What-ifs
- No consideration for the option: Dickinson governs; the request for utilities would be a counteroffer and the seller could treat the offer as rejected.
- Humble's letter said it would exercise only if utilities were provided: a conditional acceptance, which is a counteroffer; the option remains, but the later unconditional exercise would be needed.
- UCC analog: a merchant's signed firm offer under § 2-205 is irrevocable without consideration for the stated time, up to three months.
