## Facts
Hoffman, a bakery owner, negotiated with Red Owl for a franchised grocery store. Over two years its representatives assured him that $18,000 would suffice and that the deal would go through if he took certain steps: he bought and ran a small grocery to gain experience and sold it at their urging, sold his bakery, paid for an option on a lot, moved his family, and rented a house. Red Owl kept raising the capital requirement and adding terms until negotiations collapsed.

## Procedural posture
The jury found the elements of promissory estoppel and awarded damages; the court affirmed liability, affirmed most of the damages and remanded to determine the loss on the grocery store.

## Issue
Whether promissory estoppel applies to promises made in negotiations that never produced an agreement definite enough to be a contract, and how damages are measured.

## Holding
Yes. The promises were made to induce Hoffman's reliance, he relied, and injustice could be avoided only by compensation; damages were measured by his reliance expenditures, not by lost profits.

## Reasoning
Section 90 does not require all the elements of a contract; its function is to protect reliance. The court declined to limit it to promises that would otherwise be offers, and held that the remedy should be what justice requires, here the money Hoffman lost acting on Red Owl's word, since the store's profits were speculative and the franchise terms never fixed.

## Rule (own words)
You can be liable for what you led someone to spend even if you never agreed to anything definite; the measure is what they lost, not what they hoped to gain.

## What-ifs
- Red Owl's assurances were vague encouragements (we think it will work out): probably no promise sufficient for § 90.
- Hoffman had signed a letter of intent stating that neither side was bound until a franchise agreement was signed: strong defense; reliance unreasonable.
- Mexican comparison: culpa in contrahendo and liability for breaking off negotiations in bad faith reach similar reliance damages.
