## Facts
The Walkers, cattle breeders, agreed to sell Sherwood a cow, Rose 2d of Aberlone, believed by both to be barren, at a price computed by weight for beef (about $80). Before delivery the Walkers discovered she was with calf and worth $750 to $1,000 as a breeder, and refused to deliver.

## Procedural posture
Sherwood sued in replevin and won; the Supreme Court of Michigan reversed, holding the sellers could show the mutual mistake and avoid the sale.

## Issue
Whether a mutual mistake about the cow's capacity to breed allowed the sellers to rescind.

## Holding
Yes. The mistake was not about a mere quality but about the very nature of the animal: a barren cow is substantially a different creature from a breeding cow, so there was no contract for the cow actually sold.

## Reasoning
The majority distinguished mistakes going to the substance of the whole consideration from mistakes about quality, accident or value, and placed this one in the first category because the price and the purpose (beef) both rested on barrenness. The dissent argued the parties contracted about an identified cow, that both knew fertility was uncertain, and that the buyer had simply guessed better.

## Rule (own words)
Shared mistake about what the thing is, not merely what it is worth, lets the loser out, unless he took that chance when he set the price.

## What-ifs
- Only the seller believed the cow barren and the buyer suspected otherwise: unilateral mistake; § 153 requires unconscionability or the other party's knowledge of the mistake, and the buyer's suspicion may supply the latter.
- The contract said sold as is, no warranty of fertility: risk allocated by agreement under § 154(a); no rescission.
- The cow had already been delivered and slaughtered: rescission impossible; restitution questions only.
