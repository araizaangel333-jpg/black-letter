## Facts
Henry agreed to rent Krell's flat on Pall Mall for two days to view the coronation procession of Edward VII, paying a deposit and promising the balance. The King fell ill and the procession was postponed. Henry refused to pay the balance; Krell sued, and Henry counterclaimed for the deposit.

## Procedural posture
Judgment for Henry on the claim; the counterclaim was abandoned on appeal; the Court of Appeal affirmed.

## Issue
Whether the postponement of the procession excused the tenant from paying the balance.

## Holding
Yes. The procession was the foundation of the contract; its cancellation frustrated the purpose and discharged the tenant's duty to pay.

## Reasoning
The court extended Taylor v. Caldwell: the implied condition can concern not only the existence of a thing but the occurrence of a state of affairs that both parties regarded as the basis of the contract. The letting was of rooms to view the procession, not of rooms generally.

## Rule (own words)
If both sides built the deal on an event, and the event is called off, the deal is off too. If only one side cared about the event, it is just bad luck.

## What-ifs
- The lease was for a month at the ordinary rent and the tenant merely hoped to see the procession: motive, not shared purpose; no excuse.
- The contract said: rent payable whether or not the procession occurs. Express allocation of the risk; no frustration.
- The procession was postponed only after the tenant had occupied the rooms for the first day: partial frustration; restitution and apportionment questions.
