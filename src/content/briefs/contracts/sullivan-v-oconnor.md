## Facts
Alice Sullivan, a professional entertainer, was promised by Dr. O'Connor that two operations would enhance her appearance and improve her nose. After three operations her nose was worse and could not be repaired. She sued on contract and negligence theories; the jury found for her on the contract count.

## Procedural posture
The judge instructed the jury on a reliance-type measure; the plaintiff waived her exception to the instruction's limits; the surgeon's appeal was rejected and the judgment affirmed.

## Issue
What damages a patient may recover for breach of a surgeon's promise of a particular result.

## Holding
The patient could recover her out-of-pocket expenses, the worsening of her condition, and pain and suffering from the third operation; the court approved this reliance measure as proper in the circumstances while noting that expectation damages might also be available.

## Reasoning
Justice Kaplan surveyed the expectation, reliance and restitution interests, warned against excessive recoveries for optimistic medical statements, and concluded that a reliance measure that includes the detriment of unnecessary operations fairly compensates without granting the speculative value of a perfect result.

## Rule (own words)
When the promised result is hard to price, the law can instead put you back where you started: refund, the harm done, and the suffering you would not have gone through but for the promise.

## What-ifs
- The patient had evidence of lost earnings as a performer because of the worse nose: consequential expectation damages if within contemplation.
- Only two operations were performed as agreed and the result was merely not improved: reliance recovery limited to the fee and any worsening; no extra pain and suffering.
- Negligence proven: tort damages including all pain and suffering, independent of contract.
