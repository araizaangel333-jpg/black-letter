## Facts
Caldwell agreed to let Taylor use the Surrey Gardens and Music Hall on four days for concerts and fêtes, for £100 a day. Before the first date the hall was destroyed by an accidental fire. Taylor sued for the money spent advertising and preparing.

## Procedural posture
Judgment for the defendants.

## Issue
Whether the owners were liable for breach when the hall ceased to exist without their fault.

## Holding
No. The contract was subject to an implied condition that the parties would be excused if performance became impossible by the perishing of the hall.

## Reasoning
Blackburn drew on civil-law sources and English cases about contracts for personal services discharged by death: where the thing contracted about is essential to performance, the parties must have contemplated its continued existence as the foundation of the bargain, and the law implies a condition to that effect.

## Rule (own words)
If the deal cannot be done because the thing it was about is gone through nobody's fault, both sides are released. Who bears the sunk costs is a separate question.

## What-ifs
- The owners had promised to rebuild or to insure the hall for the concerts: express assumption of the risk; liable.
- The fire was caused by the owners' negligence: no excuse; fault defeats impossibility.
- A performer died before the concert: the same principle for personal-service contracts.
