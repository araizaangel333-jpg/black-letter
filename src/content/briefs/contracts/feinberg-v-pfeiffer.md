## Facts
Anna Feinberg worked for Pfeiffer for thirty-seven years. The board adopted a resolution raising her salary and promising $200 a month for life whenever she chose to retire, in recognition of her service. She kept working for a year and a half, then retired at fifty-seven and received the payments for several years. After the president died, new management questioned the arrangement and stopped paying. By then she had cancer and could not work.

## Procedural posture
She sued for the unpaid installments; judgment for her; affirmed.

## Issue
Whether the promise was supported by consideration and, if not, whether it was enforceable under promissory estoppel.

## Holding
No consideration, but enforceable under § 90 of the Restatement because she relied on the promise by retiring and the company should have expected that reliance.

## Reasoning
Past service cannot be consideration for a later promise, and the company asked nothing further of her; continuing to work was not required and retiring was her choice. But the promise was made to induce exactly that choice, she made it, and injustice could be avoided only by enforcement, given her age and health.

## Rule (own words)
Gratitude for the past is not a bargain; a promise that leads someone to quit her job is enforceable anyway if she relied on it and cannot go back.

## What-ifs
- The resolution had said: we will pay the pension if you keep working two more years. Bargain; consideration; no need for estoppel.
- She retired for reasons unrelated to the promise and could easily have found other work: reliance not substantial; § 90 may fail.
- The promise was oral and made by a manager without authority: agency and statute of frauds questions, but § 90 does not require a writing.
