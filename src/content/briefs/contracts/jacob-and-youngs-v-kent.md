## Facts
Jacob & Youngs built a country residence for Kent for about $77,000. The specifications required wrought-iron pipe of Reading manufacture; through a subcontractor's oversight, much of the pipe installed was of other brands of equal quality and price. The owner discovered it after the house was finished and the pipe encased in the walls, and demanded replacement; the builder refused and sued for the unpaid balance of about $3,500.

## Procedural posture
Directed verdict for the owner; reversed by the Appellate Division; affirmed by the Court of Appeals, 4–3.

## Issue
Whether the use of pipe of a different brand, equal in quality, was a failure of a condition to payment, and what the measure of damages was.

## Holding
The builder substantially performed; the omission was trivial and innocent, so the owner's duty to pay arose, subject to a deduction measured by the difference in value, which was nominal.

## Reasoning
Cardozo distinguished conditions from promises: parties may make any term a condition, but absent clear language courts read promises as independent where the forfeiture from treating them as conditions would be grossly out of proportion to the breach. The deviation here was insignificant in purpose and effect. As to damages, the cost of replacement would be grossly and unfairly disproportionate to the good to be attained, so the measure is the difference in value.

## Rule (own words)
Small, honest mistakes do not forfeit the price. The owner gets the money difference, and only the real cost of fixing it when that cost is not absurd.

## What-ifs
- The contract said: no payment shall be due unless Reading pipe is used throughout. Express condition; strict compliance required; no payment, though the owner may face restitution questions.
- The builder knowingly substituted cheaper pipe: willful breach; no substantial performance; the owner may withhold payment.
- The pipe was exposed and replacement cost $500: cost of completion is reasonable; damages are the $500.
