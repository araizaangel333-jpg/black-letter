## Facts
Rockingham County contracted with Luten Bridge to build a bridge as part of a planned road. After a political fight the board of commissioners resolved not to build the road and notified the company to stop work, when the company had spent about $1,900. The company kept building and finished the bridge, which stood in a forest with no road to it, and sued for about $18,300.

## Procedural posture
Judgment for the company for the full amount; reversed, with directions to limit damages.

## Issue
Whether the company could recover the cost of work done after the county repudiated.

## Holding
No. Once notified, the company's duty was to stop; it could recover the expenses incurred before the notice plus the profit it would have made, not the cost of completing an unwanted bridge.

## Reasoning
Judge Parker stated the principle that a plaintiff cannot hold a defendant liable for damages that need not have been incurred, and that after an absolute repudiation the injured party must treat the contract as broken and sue, not perform and sue for the price.

## Rule (own words)
When they tell you to stop, stop. You keep your profit and your costs to date; you do not get paid for building something nobody wants.

## What-ifs
- The county's notice came from an unauthorized official: no effective repudiation; the company could continue (the court actually had to resolve this).
- Stopping would have cost more than finishing (partly built structure that must be made safe): reasonable completion costs are not avoidable losses.
- Mexican comparison: the duty to mitigate is weak in Mexican law; a creditor who continues performing after a repudiation may still claim, which makes this rule a practical trap for Mexican lawyers in U.S. deals.
