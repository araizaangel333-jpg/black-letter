## Facts
Crunden-Martin wrote to Fairmount asking for the lowest price on ten car loads of Mason green jars, complete with caps and rubbers, with terms of delivery. Fairmount replied by letter quoting prices per gross for pints, quarts and half gallons, with a cash discount, stating the quotation was for immediate acceptance and shipment not later than May 15. Crunden-Martin telegraphed an order for ten car loads as per the letter, specifying first-quality jars. Fairmount answered that it was sold out and could not fill the order.

## Procedural posture
Crunden-Martin sued for breach and recovered; Fairmount appealed; the Court of Appeals of Kentucky affirmed.

## Issue
Whether Fairmount's letter was an offer that the telegraphed order accepted, and whether the order was too indefinite to be an acceptance.

## Holding
Yes to the first, no to the second. The letter, read in light of the specific inquiry and its language of immediate acceptance, was an offer; the order accepted it, and the specification of first-quality jars within the quoted sizes did not add a new term.

## Reasoning
A price quotation is ordinarily not an offer, but the test is what a reasonable person in the buyer's position would understand. Here the buyer had asked for a price on a stated quantity and terms; the seller answered with complete terms and asked for immediate acceptance. That is a proposal to sell, and the seller could not treat it as mere information after the buyer relied on it. On definiteness, the sizes were the seller's own options; choosing among them and asking for first quality, which the seller's quotation implied, completed rather than altered the bargain.

## Rule (own words)
Words of commitment plus a specific inquiry turn a price list into an offer. And an acceptance that fills a blank the offer left open does not vary the offer.

## What-ifs
- Fairmount's letter quoted the same prices but omitted for immediate acceptance and was sent to all its customers: much closer to Harvey, a quotation.
- The order asked for a size the quotation did not list: a counteroffer.
- The order demanded shipment by May 1 instead of May 15: a varying term, no acceptance under the mirror-image rule.
