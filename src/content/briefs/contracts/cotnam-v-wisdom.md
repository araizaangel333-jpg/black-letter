## Facts
Harrison was thrown from a streetcar and rendered unconscious; a bystander summoned Drs. Wisdom and Abel, who performed a difficult operation in an attempt to save him. He died without regaining consciousness. The physicians sued his administrator for their fee.

## Procedural posture
Judgment for the physicians; reversed and remanded because the jury had been allowed to consider the decedent's financial condition.

## Issue
Whether an obligation to pay arises without consent, and how it is measured.

## Holding
Yes, by an implied-in-law contract; the measure is the reasonable value of the services, without regard to the patient's wealth.

## Reasoning
The court explained that quasi-contracts are not contracts at all but duties the law imposes to prevent unjust enrichment, as when a lunatic or an unconscious person receives necessaries. The emergency and the surgeons' calling justified the implied obligation. But the fee must be what the services were worth, not what a rich patient might pay.

## Rule (own words)
No agreement, no promise, still a debt: when the law would be ashamed to let someone keep a benefit for free, it makes them pay what it was worth.

## What-ifs
- The patient was conscious and refused treatment: no recovery; the benefit was forced on him.
- A neighbor, not a physician, gave first aid and later billed: likely a volunteer; no recovery.
- The patient had agreed in advance to a fixed fee: express contract governs; quasi-contract is unavailable where a contract covers the subject.
