## Facts
Levi Wyman, twenty-five, returned sick from a sea voyage and was cared for by Mills for about two weeks until he died. Afterward Levi's father, Seth Wyman, wrote to Mills promising to pay the expenses. He later refused.

## Procedural posture
Mills sued on the promise; nonsuit at trial; affirmed.

## Issue
Whether a father's written promise to pay for care already given to his adult son is supported by consideration.

## Holding
No. The services were rendered before the promise and not at the father's request; the promise rested on moral obligation alone and was unenforceable.

## Reasoning
The court acknowledged the father's moral duty but held that the law enforces only promises supported by consideration or by a pre-existing legal obligation that the promise revives. The son was an adult, so the father had no legal duty to support him; the kindness of a stranger created no debt.

## Rule (own words)
A thank-you promise for a favor already done is not a contract. The exceptions are promises that bring back a debt the law once recognized.

## What-ifs
- The father had asked Mills to care for his son and promised payment beforehand: bargain, enforceable.
- The son had been a minor: the father's legal duty of support might make the promise enforceable as recognizing a legal obligation.
- Webb v. McGowin facts: the promisor himself received a material benefit; many courts and § 86 enforce the later promise.
