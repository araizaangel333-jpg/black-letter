## Facts
The Hadleys' mill stopped when its crankshaft broke. They sent the shaft by Pickford's carriers (Baxendale) to the manufacturer as a pattern for a new one; delivery was delayed several days through the carrier's neglect, and the mill stayed idle meanwhile. The Hadleys sued for the profits lost during the delay.

## Procedural posture
The jury awarded £25 for lost profits; the Court of Exchequer ordered a new trial, holding the profits were not recoverable on the facts communicated.

## Issue
Whether lost profits from the mill's stoppage were recoverable from a carrier who did not know the mill would be stopped by the delay.

## Holding
No. The only facts communicated were that the item was a broken mill shaft and the senders were millers; the stoppage of the mill was not the natural consequence of delay in most cases, and the special circumstances were not made known.

## Reasoning
Baron Alderson stated the two-branch rule and explained its policy: a carrier who knew of the special loss might have charged more or taken precautions; a party should bear only risks it could have evaluated when it contracted.

## Rule (own words)
You pay for the losses anyone would expect from your breach and for the special ones you were told about. Surprises are not your problem.

## What-ifs
- The millers told the carrier: the mill is stopped and cannot run until this shaft returns, please hurry. Special circumstances communicated; lost profits recoverable.
- The carrier's standard terms limited liability to the value of the goods: contractual limitation, usually enforceable.
- UCC sale: a buyer's consequential damages include losses from requirements the seller had reason to know of and could not reasonably be prevented by cover (§ 2-715(2)).
