## Facts
Fox contracted with Shirley MacLaine Parker to star in Bloomer Girl, a musical to be filmed in Los Angeles, for $750,000. Fox cancelled the picture and offered her the lead in Big Country, Big Man, a western to be filmed in Australia at the same pay but without the approval rights the first contract gave her. She refused and sued for the guaranteed compensation.

## Procedural posture
Summary judgment for Parker for the full amount; affirmed 6–1.

## Issue
Whether Parker's refusal of the western was a failure to mitigate that reduced her damages.

## Holding
No. The substitute was both different and inferior, so she was not required to accept it; Fox failed to show any comparable employment she unreasonably refused.

## Reasoning
The court restated the mitigation rule for wrongful discharge and held that the differences between the two roles (genre, location, loss of approvals) made the offer inferior as a matter of law. The employer, not the employee, bears the burden of showing available comparable work.

## Rule (own words)
Fire someone and you owe the salary minus what they could reasonably have earned doing the same kind of job. A worse job does not count.

## What-ifs
- Fox offered the same musical, same city, same terms, three months later: comparable; refusal would reduce damages.
- Parker took a television role for $200,000 during the period: her damages are reduced by what she earned.
- A Mexican employee dismissed without cause: constitutional severance and back pay under the LFT, with no mitigation offset of this kind.
