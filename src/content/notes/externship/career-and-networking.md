## The rules that can fail you
- Attendance at all thirteen sessions by the app; more than two unexcused absences fails the course and the externship component. Breakouts count; a reception is not an excuse.
- One hundred twenty externship hours by November 23, logged weekly in CORE ELMS with time descriptions a client could read: specific task, active verb, no confidential detail, hours in tenths.
- Mid-term (Oct. 9) and final (Dec. 4) student evaluations, read only by the program director.
- Confidentiality: nothing about the host company's matters leaves the building, including on this site and on LinkedIn. Posts about the externship describe what you learned, never what the company is doing.
- No laptops in class; a notebook. Rapid Fires at 7:30 pm the day after class.

## Networking the way the course teaches it
Three sentences in person: who you are, what you are building, why it should interest the listener (a Mexican lawyer with an LL.M., a bilingual practice for companies on both sides of the border, and a question about their work). Forty-five seconds on LinkedIn: a headline that says the same, a summary in plain English, a photo. Ask one specific question, listen, follow up within a day with one line. Sit in the front rows; speak first.

## November 18
Class photo at 5:45 at Armstrong Fieldhouse, reception 6:00–7:30. Prepare three questions for general counsels about how they work with Mexican counterparties; bring the paper's thesis as a conversation piece; collect three names to follow up with the next morning.

## Extra credit for international LL.M.s (due Dec. 11)
AI around the world (250–400 words comparing Mexico and the U.S.), your U.S. in-house toolkit (three skills, honest self-assessment, one development step each), and decoding the American GC. Small points, good writing practice, and material for the paper's conclusion.
