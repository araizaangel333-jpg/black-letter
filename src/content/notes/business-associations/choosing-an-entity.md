## The map of forms (own words)

| Form | Liability of owners | Tax | Who manages | Formed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | Unlimited | Owner's return | Owner | Nothing (maybe a DBA filing) |
| General partnership / joint venture | Unlimited, each partner binds the others | Pass-through | All partners, equal votes by default | Conduct; no filing |
| Limited partnership | GP unlimited; LPs limited while passive | Pass-through | General partner | Filing plus written agreement |
| LLP | Limited, except own or supervised misconduct | Pass-through | Partners | Registration, fee, often insurance |
| LLC | Limited | Pass-through by default; may elect corporate | Members or managers | Filing plus operating agreement |
| C corporation | Limited | Entity tax plus tax on dividends | Board elects officers | Certificate of formation |
| S corporation | Limited | Pass-through, with eligibility limits | Board | Certificate plus tax election |

## After the choice comes the drafting
Picking the entity is half the answer. The other half is the agreement that makes the relationship work: employment and salary, distributions, votes and vetoes, transfer restrictions, buy-sell triggers, what needs unanimity. Chapter 3 lives there.

## Mexican contrast
The LLC does the job of the S. de R.L. and the corporation of the S.A., but the tax logic is reversed: in Mexico every sociedad pays ISR, so pass-through is the exotic case. Here pass-through is the default for everything except the C corporation, which is why so many small businesses never incorporate.
