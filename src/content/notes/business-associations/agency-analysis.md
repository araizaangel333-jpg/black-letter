## How this shows up on the exam
The chapter scenarios and the exam essays are the same animal: a short story with several people wearing several hats (partner, employee, officer, buyer), one unauthorized or badly authorized act, and a call that asks who can recover from whom. Write the hats first, then run the checklist above for each pair of parties. Tell the story from each side ("as Rosa's lawyer I would argue…"), because the professor rewards advocacy on both sides more than a single right answer.

## The thirty-second version
Agency is consent plus control. Authority is what the principal manifested, measured from the point of view of whoever received the manifestation. Torts follow control and scope. Everything else (ratification, estoppel, laches) is a way the principal gets stuck after the fact.

## Mexican contrast
In Mexico the question is almost always what the written power of attorney says (CCF art. 2554 and the categories of poderes). Here the written document matters first too, but conduct creates authority just as well, and apparent authority protects the third party much more strongly than any Mexican doctrine of apariencia.
