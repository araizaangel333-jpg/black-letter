## The design
Monday and Wednesday classes that pair a chapter of A Lawyer Writes with a Bluebook topic and an ICW chapter, plus short writing assignments that grow into the memo: the IRAC negligence scenario (September), the I/CREAC paragraph on a criminal case with a rubric and a peer review (early October), the IIED research chart (October 9), the Discussion section (October 26), and the full memo (November 2). The professor said in October that the focus is mechanics first; the memo is graded on mechanics and substance, and it is the piece that gets individual feedback.

## What is left
- October 28: Conclusion and editing (chapters 15 and 16). Run the editing checklist on the Discussion you already submitted.
- November 2: full memo due; class on professional emails (chapter 18); ICW chapter 8.
- November 4: no class; ICW chapters 10 and 12.
- November 9: common mistakes and citation review; ICW chapters 13 and 15.
- November 11: no class; one-on-one sessions begin. Book one as soon as the memo comes back.

## How to use this site for the memo
1. Briefs of the eight cases the professor listed, with pincites to verify in the opinions.
2. The memo anatomy, CREAC, explaining and applying checklists, in that order.
3. The citation drills and the Texas citation checklist the night before submitting.
4. The writing lab exercises: Question Presented and Brief Answer, Statement of Facts, the editing drill and the email drill, all on the salon facts, so you practice the form without touching the graded problem.

## For the one-on-one
Bring three questions, not the whole memo: one on structure (did my roadmap work?), one on a rule explanation you were unsure about, one on English (a pattern the feedback flagged). Ask what the strongest counter-analysis would have been.
