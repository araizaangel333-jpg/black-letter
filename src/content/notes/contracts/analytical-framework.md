## Classical versus modern, in one paragraph
Classical contract law (late nineteenth century, Williston) treats the contract as a private act of will policed by formal rules: offer and acceptance that mirror each other, consideration as the sole test of enforceability, strict conditions, damages limited to the bargain. Modern contract law (Corbin, Llewellyn and the UCC, the Restatement Second) adds reliance as a second source of obligation, lets courts fill gaps and imply good faith, relaxes formalities for merchants, and asks about fairness and the parties' reasonable expectations. The course moves from the first to the second within each topic; the exam rewards knowing which rule is which.

## Mandatory and persuasive authority
A Texas court is bound by the Texas Supreme Court, the Texas courts of appeals in its district, and the Texas Business and Commerce Code; the Restatement, other states' cases and the Uniform Commercial Code's official comments persuade. On the exam, state the rule as the majority rule or the Restatement rule unless the question names a state.
