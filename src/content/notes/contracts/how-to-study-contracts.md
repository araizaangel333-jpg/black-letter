## The rhythm
Tuesday and Thursday 8:15–10:15 in 207 Florence Hall; a reading assignment sheet arrives by email each week with casebook pages, Restatement and UCC sections and the professor's own handouts (case time lines, analyses, discussion notes); Wednesday 3:15–4:15 review sessions since September 30 work through old exam questions with the professor's issue outlines; the assignment sheets tell you when to outline (the weekend after each unit closes). Office hours Tuesday and Thursday 10:15–12:00 in 301A Storey Hall or by email.

## The exam
One four-hour essay exam, open book and open notes, Monday December 7, 1:30–5:30 pm; no AI or internet. Participation counts only in borderline cases. The course outline runs: introduction; formation (classical and modern, quasi-contract, UCC); enforceability defenses; the law of the contract (parol evidence, modification, interpretation); conditions and justified nonperformance; excuse (briefly); remedies.

## What the sample questions show
- Point values and suggested minutes on every question (7, 8, 12, 15 points; 20 to 40 minutes). Budget time by points.
- Fact patterns built on the UCC: oral sales of goods, confirming letters, cancellations, warranties with limitation clauses, §§ 2-201 and 2-725 above all. Know the Code sections by number.
- Short conceptual questions worth 1 to 4 points each: distinguish two doctrines, state what additional showing a defense requires, criticize a famous case, resolve a tension between two Restatement sections, say who bears the burden of proof.
- Instructions like discuss fully versus very briefly discuss: match the length to the words and the points.
- The grading outline gives points per issue and per argument on each side; a conclusion without the counterargument earns half.

## The study plan for the last four weeks
1. Keep the outline current after each unit (the professor's own cadence): defenses now, then interpretation, then conditions, excuse and remedies as they are covered.
2. Redo every review-session question against the issue outline, timed, with the book closed first and open second.
3. Use the quiz sets on this site by topic, then the exam-style short answers; use the writing lab's exam problems with the point checklists.
4. Build a one-page UCC map (§§ 1-303, 2-104, 2-105, 2-201, 2-204 to 2-209, 2-302, 2-306, 2-313 to 2-315, 2-601, 2-609, 2-610, 2-615, 2-703 to 2-718, 2-725) and a one-page Restatement map (§§ 20, 24, 33, 36–63, 69, 71–90, 110, 131, 151–157, 159–169, 201–206, 224–245, 250–257, 261–265, 344–356).
5. For the open-book format, tabs matter more than memory: index the outline by issue and by section number.
