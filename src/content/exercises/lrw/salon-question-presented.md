## Assignment
Using the salon facts from the companion exercise (Renata Solís, the twenty-two false reviews posted by former employee Kyle Benner over six weeks, the eight midnight hang-up calls over two weeks, the lost bookings, the doctor's visit and the anxiety prescription), write the Question Presented and the Brief Answer for a memo whose Discussion concluded that the reviews cannot support IIED and the calls might, if outrageous enough.

Two parts, each under 100 words. Then compare with the model.

## Model answer
Question Presented

Under Texas law, can a salon owner recover for intentional infliction of emotional distress against a former employee who posted twenty-two false online reviews attacking her professional reputation over six weeks and, separately, called her personal phone eight times between midnight and 2 a.m. over two weeks and hung up each time?

Brief Answer

Probably not as to the reviews, and doubtful as to the calls. In Texas, intentional infliction of emotional distress is a gap-filler tort that is unavailable when the gravamen of the complaint is another tort such as defamation, even if that other claim is weak. The reviews attack reputation, so a court will treat them as defamation territory and dismiss the IIED claim based on them. The calls are conduct rather than speech, so they can be examined under the four elements (intentional or reckless, extreme and outrageous, causing severe distress); eight hang-up calls over two weeks are unlikely to be found extreme and outrageous, though the medical evidence helps on severity.

## Common mistakes
- Writing a question that could be asked of any IIED case (no facts).
- Loading the question with adjectives (vicious campaign, harassing calls).
- Starting the Brief Answer with the rule or a citation instead of the answer.
- Answering the reviews and the calls together when the Discussion treated them separately.
