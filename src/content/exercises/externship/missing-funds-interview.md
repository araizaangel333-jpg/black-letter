## Facts
You are general counsel of a Dallas logistics company. On Monday the CFO tells you that $180,000 is missing from a regional account and that the branch manager, Teresa, "probably moved it." The CFO wants you to interview Teresa this afternoon, "as a friend, so she opens up," and to email him a summary "for the board." The company has no written investigation policy.

## Call of the question
In no more than 250 words, write the email you would send the CFO before the interview, bottom line up front: what you will and will not do, how you will open the interview, what will be privileged, and what else must happen today.

## Model answer
Bottom line: I will interview Teresa today, but as the company's lawyer, not as her friend, and I will not send a summary to the board until we agree on scope. Here is why and what happens first.

The company is my client (Model Rule 1.13), not you and not Teresa. If I let her believe I represent her, anything she tells me may be unusable and I may be disqualified. I will open with an Upjohn warning in plain words: I represent the company; this conversation is privileged, but the privilege belongs to the company, which may share what she says with regulators or law enforcement; she may want her own lawyer.

What is protected: my communications with employees for the purpose of giving the company legal advice, and my notes and analysis prepared because litigation is likely (work product). What is not protected: the underlying facts, the bank records, and any business advice mixed into my memo. For that reason my report will go to you and the audit committee chair as a legal assessment, in one document, marked privileged, and I will not copy the whole board by email.

Before 3 p.m. I will issue a litigation hold to accounting, IT and the branch: no deletion of emails, messages or ledger entries. If the facts point to a crime or to anyone in finance, I will recommend outside counsel to run the investigation, so that its independence cannot be attacked later.

Risks: tipping Teresa before records are preserved; an interview that looks like entrapment; a board summary that waives privilege. Each is avoidable if we do the steps in this order.

## Common mistakes
- Agreeing to interview "as a friend"; the warning exists precisely to prevent that.
- Promising the employee confidentiality the company does not control.
- Emailing the whole board a narrative summary, which risks waiver.
- Interviewing before preserving the records.
